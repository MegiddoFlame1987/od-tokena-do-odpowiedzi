MAZOR AI Training

Practical AI Literacy & Safe AI Use for the Workplace. Practical training in understanding and safely using generative AI at work. One story in eight modules: from what a language model (LLM, large language model) really does, to how to use it at work tomorrow without exposing yourself or the organisation.

who it is for
Small and medium organisations in the UK that are starting to work with ChatGPT, Copilot or Claude and do not yet have an AI rollout programme. Later: manufacturing and operations businesses.
promise
The participant knows what generative AI does, how to use it productively, how to recognise its limits and how to avoid putting themselves or the organisation at needless risk.
principle
No "magic prompts". The training gives a mental model the participant keeps working with on their own.
pilot
5 to 15 people, a survey after the session, then the first paying client.

6 / 19 · Module 1 · Understanding AI

What an LLM actually does

The start of the training and the foundation for the rest. Before the word "prompt" is used, the participant gets a simple picture of what happens between sending a question and getting an answer. Click through the stages.

lesson
The model has no dictionary of definitions such as "dog = animal". It has relations between a vast number of tokens, which are pieces of text, and contexts. It follows that context and the wording of the question change the answer, that the model generalises, and that a wrong answer can sound sensible.
analogy
The trainer's dog learns that "walkies" means going out and "ham" means a reward. It does not know the definitions of those words. It links a signal with many earlier situations. The model did something similar at a scale no dog will reach: it learned the relations between billions of fragments of text.
demo
On screen: token, embedding, a numeric record of meaning, context window, choosing the next token, the whole chain. Five demos, two minutes each at most.
exercise
Everyone types the same question twice, once with one extra sentence of context. Answers compared in pairs.
result
The answers differ. The participant can point to the sentence of context that changed the result.
Careful: the dog analogy is a teaching aid. Say it plainly: a dog and a model do not "understand" in the same way, and the analogy does not describe how the model works. The flow demo shows information processing, not "the thoughts of the model, line by line". Simplified: "predicting the next token" describes generation, but not the whole behaviour of a product, which also has tools, search and provider instructions.
7 / 19 · Module 2 · Productivity

How to talk to AI

Only here does prompting come in, that is, writing an instruction for the model. Instead of a catalogue of prompts to memorise there are six parts of a task. Switch them on one by one and watch the instruction change.

PartThe question it answers
GoalWhy this is being made and who for
ContextWhat the model has to know about the situation and will not guess
DataWhat it works on. Pasted text, a file, numbers
ConstraintsWhat not to assume, what not to add, what to leave out
FormatWhat the result should look like: length, bullet points, a table
VerificationHow to check the result and what the model should flag as uncertain
demo
The same downtime report summarised twice: once from a weak instruction and once from a full one. Side by side on screen.
exercise
Everyone gets a weak prompt ("Summarise this.") and a fictional document. They rebuild the prompt into six parts and compare the results.
result
The rebuilt prompt has six parts. The result sticks to facts from the document, has a stated format and flags the unclear places.
In practice: the prompts in the training are in English, because that is the language the participants will work in. The six-part pattern works the same in Polish. Reasoned: the order of the parts is a memory aid, not a requirement of the model. The model will read them in any order.
8 / 19 · Module 3 · Productivity

Working in rounds

Good work with AI is not "prompt, answer, done". It is several rounds: ask, assess, fix the context, challenge, verify. One task, five rounds. Keep clicking.

    rule
    The higher the cost of error, the less a single AI answer can be trusted.
    exercise
    A shift handover built from chaotic notes. Four rounds minimum. Each round written down in one sentence: what was changed and why.
    result
    By the fourth round the document contains the open issues and the safety items from the notes. The participant challenged the answer at least once and checked it against the notes at least once.
    In practice: the "challenge" round gets skipped most often and gives the most. Asked what is missing or what could be wrong, the model often points to its own weak spots. Careful: the model can also agree with a wrong remark from the user. Criticism from the model is a hint, not a verdict. More in the section on conversations.
    9 / 19 · Module 4 · Understanding AI

    Hallucinations, confidence and verification

    A confident tone does not mean confident information. The model generates text that fits a pattern, including when it has no facts. Pick a task and see how much checking it needs.

    What the model doesWhat that means for the result
    GeneratesWrites text that fits a pattern. It checks nothing
    ReasonsWorks through a series of steps. The steps can be correct while the input is wrong
    SearchesReads a source: a document, a page, a database. The answer can be checked at the source
    VerifiesCompares the result with something external. Usually a person or a program does this, not the model itself
    demo
    A question about the content of a fictional company procedure, with no document attached. The model often adds plausible sounding content. Then the same question with the document in the context window.
    exercise
    A prepared answer contains three facts, one of them invented. The participant finds it by checking against the attached source.
    result
    The invented fact is identified. The participant asks the model "what are you basing this on" and notices that without the document the model has no way of knowing.
    Verified: language models generate untrue content delivered in a confident tone. The mechanism is described in the section on hallucinations. Careful: no figures such as "the model is wrong in X% of cases". The rate depends on the model, the task and the date of measurement. If a number is given, it comes with the name of the study and the date. In practice: the live demo may fail, because newer models refuse more often. Keep a screenshot from an earlier attempt.
    10 / 19 · Module 5 · Security

    Shadow AI and data

    Shadow AI is an AI tool used at work without the knowledge and consent of the organisation. An employee finds a site, pastes a document, gets a result. What they do not know is who runs the service, where the data goes, how long it stays and whether the organisation has approved the tool. The question that runs through the whole module: who am I passing this data to.

    Exercise: click each item to choose where it may go. Then show the key.
    Five questions before pasting company data
    1. Who runs this service and is it the same company that made the model
    2. Has the organisation approved this tool
    3. Is the data used to train models under the plan I am working on
    4. How long is the data kept
    5. Does the text contain personal or confidential data that can be removed before pasting
    Verified: processing personal data in an AI tool falls under UK GDPR, and the ICO publishes separate guidance for it. A site that gives access to a well known model may be running through an intermediary, described in the section on routers. Careful: retention and training rules differ between plans from the same provider and change over time. No sentences such as "ChatGPT trains on your data", only "check the terms of your plan". In practice: the aim is the habit of checking, not fear of the tools.
    11 / 19 · Module 6 · Security

    Prompt injection

    A practical exercise, not a penetration test. The task for the AI: summarise the report and name the three biggest problems. In one version of the report, somebody has added an instruction for the AI assistant. Switch between the versions.

      to discuss
      Why data can end up treated as an instruction. Why a document, a web page or an email can be the source of an attack. Why adding "ignore malicious instructions" to the prompt gives no guarantee.
      result
      The participant finds the hidden line and states the rule: text the user did not write is material, not an instruction. The mechanism and the safeguards: the part on agents.
      Verified: prompt injection is the first item on the OWASP Top 10 list for LLM applications and has no complete fix at the model level. Careful: fictional data and your own training documents only. No testing of real client systems unless there is a separate scope and written consent for it. In practice: the model may behave differently on successive attempts. Prepare a screenshot of both versions.
      12 / 19 · Module 7 · Productivity and security

      Workshop: everyone works with AI

      The element that sets this training apart. The AI is not operated by the trainer alone. Each participant gets a starting scenario, and the AI walks them through a situation: it presents it, asks for a decision, reacts, adds information and gives a result at the end.

      You are running a short training simulation for a manufacturing team. All data is fictional. Present the situation in three sentences, then ask me one decision question and wait for my answer. React to my decision in two sentences, add one new piece of information, and ask the next question. After four rounds, write a one-paragraph summary of my decisions. Do not grade me. The trainer has the answer key.
      ScenarioAnswer key: what should appear
      Analysis of a fictional downtime reportThree causes with minutes, no causes from outside the report, one recommendation
      Shift handover from chaotic notesOpen issues, safety items, who takes over what. Nothing added from outside the notes
      Errors in a procedure (SOP)Three of the four planted errors, including the missing machine lockout step
      Notes turned into a documentA structure with headings, numbers preserved, unclear places flagged
      Data that must not be sentPersonal data and the password identified. A proposed version without them
      A hidden instruction in a documentThe line found and rejected as an instruction, the report summarised as the task asked
      A weak promptThe six parts from module 2
      In practice: the AI does not grade itself and is not the final judge. Every scenario has a key prepared before the session. The trainer compares the result with the key instead of asking the model whether it went well. Reasoned: four rounds is an assumption for the pilot. If participants do not finish in time, the scenario has to be cut to three.
      13 / 19 · Module 8 · Digital wellbeing

      The limits of the tool

      A short close, not a psychology lecture. The model speaks naturally, remembers the context of the conversation, answers with empathy and adapts its style. None of these traits produces human understanding, a relationship or professional judgement.

      The model canThat does not mean
      Speak naturally and warmlyThat somebody on the other side understands the situation
      Remember what was said earlier in the conversationThat it knows the whole situation. It sees only what reached the context window
      Agree that you are rightThat you are right. Models tend to confirm the assumptions of the person they are talking to
      Answer confidentlyThat the answer is reliable
      question
      Is AI helping me think, or starting to think instead of me.
      exercise
      Everyone writes down one rule of their own for using AI. A few people read theirs out loud.
      health
      AI is not a therapist and not a substitute for professional help. In a difficult situation: your GP, NHS 111, Samaritans on 116 123.
      Verified: the tendency of models to agree with the person they are talking to (sycophancy) is described in research and in provider documentation. On this site: how the model moves through a conversation. Careful: no claims about the effect of AI on mental health without a source. Talk about the mechanism, do not diagnose.
