Did AI Make This?

This question comes up more and more often. The answer is not the one people expect. There is no tool that looks at the content and says who made it. There are three mechanisms, each one checks something different and each one has a different weakness. This page shows what is left of them by the time a file reaches you. And how to answer when the origin cannot be established.

Part I

Three mechanisms

An AI detector is a program meant to recognise content made by AI. Three different things hide behind that name. Most wrong conclusions come from mixing them up.

1 / 12

What is actually being checked

Two of these mechanisms read something that somebody deliberately left in the file. The third guesses from the content alone. Click to see the difference.

Verified: these are three separate technologies, not three versions of the same one. The first two answer yes or nothing. The third always gives some percentage, even when it knows nothing.
2 / 12

The watermark sits in the content

A watermark sits in the pixels themselves or in the sound. Metadata is a description attached beside the file, for example a date and the name of a program. A watermark is not attached, so it does not vanish when the file is saved in another format.

OperationMetadataWatermark
Sending through a messaging appGoneSurvives
ScreenshotGoneUsually survives
Compression, change of formatUsually goneSurvives
Heavy cropping, repaintingGoneMay be gone
Verified: a watermark survives compression and saving in another format, metadata does not. Simplified: the dots in the drawing are only a picture. A real watermark is a small change of values spread across the whole file.
3 / 12

The record: Content Credentials

The second mechanism is a signed record attached to the file. It stores what made the file and what happened to it afterwards. The standard is called C2PA. In software it usually appears as Content Credentials.

  • 1Generated in an AI tool. The record stores the tool name and the date, the whole thing signed.
  • 2Opened in an editor, colours adjusted. The editor adds another entry and signs it with its own key.
  • 3Sent by email as an attachment. The record travels with the file.
  • 4The recipient takes a screenshot. The whole history is lost. A new file appears with no past.

Strength: the signature can be checked, so the record is hard to forge. Weakness: it is as easy to remove as deleting a text file. A missing record means nothing.

Verified: C2PA is an open standard with a digital signature. Some cameras and editors support it. In practice: most files online have no record, because platforms strip it.
Part II

What survives the journey

A file rarely reaches you straight from the generator. On the way it loses exactly the traces that are easiest to read.

Verified: social media services and messaging apps strip metadata on sending. A screenshot creates a new file with no history. Simplified: whether the watermark disappears at stage 4 depends on how heavily the file was edited.

Why no trace means nothing

Four files and the same result four times: nothing found. Something different sits behind it each time. Click a file.

The most important sentence on this page: a negative result says only that no trace was found. It does not say that there was no trace, and it does not say who made the file.
Part III

Tools that really work

There are two and they work on image, video and audio. Both have a narrow scope. Both are worth using, but neither gives certainty.

6 / 12

SynthID Detector

SynthID is a watermark inserted by AI tools from Google and its partners. SynthID Detector is the Google tool that reads that watermark. Since October 2026 anybody can use it at synthid.com. It checks image, video and audio, not text.

What it readsThe SynthID watermark in image, video and audio

Does it read textNo. Not even text from the Gemini app, although that is sometimes watermarked

Is there an APIThere is no public API, that is, no access for other programs. You upload the file by hand

Verified, state of play on 8 October 2026: access for everybody, the file types, the daily limit and the lack of text support. Source: the Google announcement and the tool description. Needs refreshing: the partner list is growing.
7 / 12

Reading metadata on your own machine

Before you send a file to somebody else's tool, read its metadata. You do this locally, that is, on your own computer, with no limit and without sending the file anywhere. There are four possible results.

Verified: these fields exist and can be read locally. They are the C2PA Content Credentials record, the IPTC source marker in XMP, text blocks in PNG and the Software field in EXIF, that is, the name of the program. Note: each of them can be written in by hand, so result B is circumstantial evidence, not proof.
Part IV

Text

There is no good news here. Text cannot be checked. Tools that claim otherwise do real harm.

8 / 12

Why text detectors fail

A text detector measures how easy the next words are to predict. The trouble is that people often write very predictably, and text from a model, from ChatGPT for example, stops being predictable after a few edits. Click a sample.

Verified: text detectors give false alarms. They hit boilerplate texts and people writing in a foreign language most often. Simplified: the percentages in the demo are an example of a pattern, not a measurement of any particular tool.
9 / 12

What to do instead

When the question is whether somebody wrote something themselves, asking about the tool leads nowhere. One thing works: a conversation about the content.

Instead ofDo thisWhy it works
Pasting the text into a detectorAsk for the drafts and the file historyWork on a text leaves traces that cannot be faked after the fact
Looking for proof in the textAsk the author about the content: where this number came from, what this paragraph meansThe author of their own text answers straight away
Settling it after the factSet a rule for the futureA clear rule settles the next twenty cases
Asking "did you use AI"Ask "what in this document is yours"The second can be answered honestly. The first forces a defence
Opinion, not measurement: this is a conclusion from how the mechanism works, not the result of a study. Thresholds and company rules have to be set by you.
Part V

Procedure

What to do when the question actually comes up. And how to write the answer down so that nobody reads more into it than it says.

10 / 12

Decision tree

Answer the questions. At the end you get a next step, not a verdict.

Rule of the tree: no branch ends with the sentence "a human made this". No available tool supports that conclusion.
11 / 12

How to write down the result

The biggest risk is not in the tool. It is in the sentence you write after it. The same result can be written down honestly or dishonestly.

Do not writeWrite
I checked, this is not AINo watermark and no metadata were found. This does not settle the origin
The detector showed 87%, so it is AIText detectors do not give a result I base a decision on
The photo is authenticThe photo has camera metadata, consistent with the stated date
He used AIThe file contains metadata from tool X. I do not know who added it

The report template has four rows. You always fill in the last one, even when the result is certain.

File: name Verdict: letter and one sentence Verified: metadata yes/no, SynthID yes/no What I do not know: ...
In practice: without the last row, in six months the note can serve as proof. Proof of something it never stated.
12 / 12

When it is about a person

The result is about the file. The decision is about a person. Between the two there is a gap that no tool closes.

ResultWhat you may say about the fileWhat you may not say about the person
Watermark foundA tool that inserts this watermark took part in making itThat this person used it, or that they knew what the file was
Metadata points to a generatorThe file contains such an entryThat the entry is true. It can be written in within a minute
Nothing foundNo traces were foundNothing. In either direction
Text detectorNothingNothing. That result is not used against people
Plain opinion: a false accusation costs a company more than undetected use of AI. Especially when it rests on a tool that sounds more certain than it is. An accusation destroys trust and can become a legal case. Undetected use is usually fixed by a rule for the future.
